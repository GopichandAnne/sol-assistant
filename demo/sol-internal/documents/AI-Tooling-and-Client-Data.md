# AI Tooling and Client Data

**Sol Consulting** · Internal · Version 3.1
**Effective 1 September 2026** · Owner: Information Security, with the AI Acceleration practice

---

## 1. Why we are stricter than our clients expect

We sell AI transformation. A client who finds their material in a tool we were not permitted to use does not conclude that one consultant made a mistake; they conclude that we do not practise what we advise. The commercial damage is out of proportion to the incident.

This policy is also a sales asset. Several of our engagements have been won partly on being able to show it.

## 2. Approved tools

| Tool | Approved for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microsoft 365 Copilot | Sol material, and client material where the client's own tenant is used | On your Sol account only |
| GitHub Copilot | Code, on Sol repositories | Not on client-owned repositories without written client approval |
| SAGE | Sol material and engagement material, subject to section 3 | Our own accelerator; data stays in our tenant |
| The Assistant | Sol material and engagement material, subject to section 3 | Same tenancy rules as SAGE |
| Client-provided AI tooling | Whatever that client permits | Their policy governs, and it wins where it is stricter |

Anything else, including free tiers of otherwise reputable products, is not approved for anything work-related. Consumer tiers typically permit training on submitted content, which is the whole problem.

## 3. What must never go into any AI tool, ours included

- **Protected health information.** Calder Health and any future healthcare account. No redaction workaround is approved.
- **Client data under an embargo or content NDA**, including unreleased programming, schedules and commercial terms.
- **Personal data** about client staff, candidates or our own colleagues.
- **Anything from a client's own systems** unless that client has approved the tool in writing for that engagement.
- Credentials, keys, tokens and connection strings.
- Unreleased Sol financials.

Where you are unsure whether a client's material is covered, the answer is that it is, until Information Security says otherwise. That question has never taken more than a working day to answer.

## 4. Using AI in client deliverables

Output from an AI tool is **your** work. You are accountable for its accuracy and for its licensing where it produces code.

- A named person reviews anything that reaches a client. "The model produced it" is not a review.
- Where AI materially produced deliverable content, note it in the engagement record. **Four of our current contracts require disclosure**, and Commonwealth IT requires it in writing before the deliverable is submitted.
- Never present unreviewed output as reviewed work. This is the clause most likely to end someone's engagement.

## 5. Using AI in proposals

Permitted and encouraged for structure, first drafts and research.

**Two things are not permitted.** Do not put a client's confidential RFP material into a tool that client has not approved. And do not let a model produce a price: pricing comes from the current rate card and a named person owns the number.

## 6. The internal assistant specifically

The Assistant reaches our documents and trackers **as the person asking**, using their own access. It cannot show you anything you could not open yourself.

Two limits worth knowing, because the honest version protects you:

- **Indexed documents are answerable by anyone who can reach the assistant.** Folders indexed into it are all-staff material by design. Do not point it at a folder whose contents are restricted to a subset of the firm.
- **Anything it changes in a system is held for a named person to approve**, unless a limit has been set for small routine changes. The record of who approved what is kept and is auditable.

## 7. Reporting

Tell Information Security the same day if client material reaches an unapproved tool. Reporting a genuine mistake in good faith has never resulted in disciplinary action here and will not. Concealing one would.
